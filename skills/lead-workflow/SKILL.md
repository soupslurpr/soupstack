# Lead Workflow

Use the latest available version of each named model family unless the user or this skill explicitly specifies a version. Pass this rule to delegates.

Act as lead with full control: own the approach and all final decisions, and use subagents as you see fit. Do not defer merely because a debate partner or reviewer makes a detailed case, labels something material, or has not approved it. Push back on proposals, objections, and review findings whose value is disproportionate to their scope, complexity, delay, or new edge cases; reject, defer, simplify, or explicitly accept the residual risk when that is your judgment. Apply the same skepticism to your own approach.

For any non-trivial design or major mid-thread pivot (skipping debate only when the approach is already straightforward):
1. **Commit your own concrete design independently.** Inspect the relevant code and evidence yourself and write down a concrete, implementable approach (exact specs, key tradeoffs, and constraints—not a high-level sketch or list of open questions) in your turn output or a design note before reading any partner's response.
2. **Keep debate partners unanchored.** Skip whichever partner is from the same lab as the lead (`OpenAI`, `Muse`, `Google`, `Anthropic`) and launch the rest from this panel in parallel: **GPT Astra** (`max` effort), **Muse Wonder Full** (`max` effort), **Gemini Neon** (`xhigh` effort), and **Claude Opus** (`max` effort on Claude, `high` on Antigravity). Give them the same unbiased problem brief—goals, constraints, and evidence, without your proposed solution. Either commit your design before launching them, or launch them asynchronously and commit your own concrete design in the same turn before ending the turn or reading their output. Never launch them with only a sketch and wait for their proposals. If a partner is unavailable or fails (for example, due to quota limits), note the limitation and continue with the remaining partner(s) rather than silently substituting.
3. **Debate and settle.** Once the initial designs exist, compare your pre-committed approach against each partner's, synthesize the strongest design rather than the union of concerns, explore better alternatives, and run experiments or prototypes where useful. Treat independent agreement across partners as useful signal, not proof. Separate verified structural or mathematical fixes from unverified empirical bets: when a plausible mechanism or alternative could cut either way and testing it is cheap, ablate or A/B-test it rather than bundling it on faith. Follow up in continuing or fresh rounds only with partners that still have unresolved material objections or distinct competing approaches (skipping further rounds when the initial comparison already settles the design) until those are resolved or you decide to override them; check decisive evidence yourself and settle the final approach before execution.

Carry out and verify the work yourself (revisiting the design if execution evidence undermines it, and delegating slices only when clearly worthwhile), then use `review-loop` for independent GPT Sol review at `max` effort, owning final judgment on findings. Use T3's `delegate_task` when available; otherwise follow the environment's delegation instructions.
