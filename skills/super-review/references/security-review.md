# Security assessment within the super review

Use this guidance for phases 2 and 3. The current user request remains the objective; security assessment does not erase the broader review or its requested checks. Give each fresh discovery delegate a bounded target, useful starting points, applicable permissions and limits, and a request for examined flows, methods, evidence, and gaps. Let it choose the questions and methods. A targeted validation brief may name a suspected issue only when the user asked or prior evidence supports it; give that evidence and provenance. Do not require a finding count.

Keep one compact evidence record with pointers to target version or state, observed and reported behavior, source or model results, methods, prerequisites, counterevidence, and unanswered questions. Preserve earlier reports as evidence with their own status. Share only material permitted for the chosen worker, route, transcript, and artifacts.

Use [the triage criteria](triage.md) for prioritization and claim validation. Before accepting a consequential finding, have a fresh reviewer inspect its underlying evidence and try to refute both the behavior and its claimed impact. For broad work, obtain independent coverage review while it can still trigger follow-up: search accessible target material beyond the investigators' inventory, inspect representative underlying evidence, and identify consequential gaps with starting points. Carry feasible necessary work forward. Renew closure review after substantive follow-up; a finite, evidence-bounded conclusion may leave justified residual uncertainty.

Before temporary changes, capture enough permitted baseline detail and an authorized restoration path. After interruption, inspect completed work and continuing processes before overlapping work or cleanup. Pass revised limits to affected delegates and verify prohibited activity stops. Preserve partially completed evidence. Apply the lead workflow's recovery and provider limits to refused delegated work.
